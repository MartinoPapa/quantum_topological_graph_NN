\chapter*{Abstract}

The rapid expansion of digital financial ecosystems has generated increasingly complex and high-dimensional transaction networks, making the detection of fraudulent activities a critical yet formidable challenge. As illicit actors employ sophisticated strategies such as fraud rings and cyclic transfers to obscure their trails, traditional heuristic and rule-based detection systems frequently fall short. 

The primary goal of this thesis is to develop a detailed Python implementation of a novel Quantum Topological Graph Neural Network (QTGNN) designed specifically for fraud detection. By synergizing quantum state embedding, graph neural networks, and topological data analysis, the QTGNN aims to capture higher-order, long-range dependencies and robust topological invariants that are highly indicative of coordinated fraudulent behaviors. 

To evaluate the model's efficacy and adaptability, the implementation will be tested across a variety of structural challenges using four distinct datasets: the Elliptic Bitcoin Dataset, Ethereum Phishing Transaction Networks, IBM AMLSim, and PaySim. Furthermore, to balance computational feasibility with practical applicability, the model will be trained on a quantum simulator, while the final testing phase will be executed on actual quantum hardware to optimize resource costs.
